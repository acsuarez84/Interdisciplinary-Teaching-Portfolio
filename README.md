# Interdisciplinary Teaching Portfolio

**Intersectional Machines: A Feminist Digital Humanities Approach to AI**
Angely Suarez DeJesus · ENG 6813 Interdisciplinary Teaching

A course design asking one question: how does generative AI shape who we are?
Students read feminist and intersectional theory, work with generative AI directly
and log every interaction in a prompt journal, then build something that argues
from what they found. Reading, writing and making are treated as one continuous
practice. Every student makes something — there is no writing-only path through
the capstone.

## The course

ENG4237 is an upper-division course for juniors and seniors who have completed
first-year composition and at least one 2000-level literature, writing or digital
humanities course. No technical background is assumed, and no prior coursework in
digital humanities or feminist theory.

Seventeen calendar weeks across four units, each journal checkpoint landing on a
unit boundary. The objectives climb deliberately — Remember, Understand, Apply,
Analyze, Evaluate, Create — and the four units follow the same arc.

## Reading list

Section 4 of the syllabus lists only what the weekly schedule actually assigns,
with project readings in separate labelled sections. Section 5 is a menu students
choose from, not a queue they must clear. A syllabus that advertises 18 texts and
teaches 13 trains students not to trust it.

## Grade structure

1000 points. The journal is graded once, at its three checkpoints.

| Component | Weight | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Low-Stakes 1 — Textual Map | 5% | 50 |
| Journal Submissions 1–3 | 30% | 300 |
| Weekly reflections + peer responses | 10% | 100 |
| Final artifact (capstone) | 45% | 450 |
| Written response | 10% | 100 |
| **Total** | **100%** | **1000** |
